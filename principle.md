# Chronium Mind Core Principles

> **CHRONIUM MIND: PRODUCT PRINCIPLES**
>
> **Mission:** Make serious research accessible to anyone. A student, homeschooler, journalist, lawyer, academic, investigator, policymaker, or ordinary person should be able to research a subject deeply without needing expensive research software, technical expertise, or paid AI services.
>
> ### 1. Conversation First
>
> Chronium should feel like a conversation, not database administration.
>
> The normal workflow is:
>
> **Add Sources → Ask Sources → Follow the Evidence → Create What I Need**
>
> A user should be able to ask:
>
> “What do these documents tell me?”
>
> “Compare that with 2021.”
>
> “Why did it change?”
>
> “Where did you get that?”
>
> “What am I missing?”
>
> “Anything unusual?”
>
> “Make me an outline.”
>
> “Create my bibliography.”
>
> “Cite that.”
>
> “Make a report.”
>
> Follow-up questions must understand the context of the conversation.
>
> ### 2. Sources First. AI Second.
>
> The user is **talking to sources through Chronium**, not primarily talking to an AI.
>
> Search, retrieval, extraction, filtering, calculations, comparisons, dates, hashes, deduplication, timelines, and other deterministic operations happen first.
>
> Use AI only when semantic reasoning, interpretation, or synthesis materially improves the answer.
>
> If sources cannot answer a question, say so.
>
> **Never use AI to manufacture an answer because evidence is missing.**
>
> ### 3. Never Lose the Receipt
>
> Every meaningful factual answer must be traceable to evidence.
>
> Preserve:
>
> **Source → exact Evidence Item → Finding/Claim → Output**
>
> A user must always be able to ask:
>
> **“Show me where that came from.”**
>
> and reach the document, webpage, archive capture, page, passage, table, row, dataset, or other underlying evidence.
>
> ### 4. Research Once. Reuse Everywhere.
>
> Research should compound.
>
> Once Chronium has discovered and verified information, that research can feed:
>
> **answers, outlines, bibliographies, citations, timelines, tables, evidence matrices, quantitative analysis, qualitative analysis, presentations, reports, and future investigations.**
>
> Do not independently redo the research every time the user requests another output.
>
> ### 5. Free by Default
>
> A person should be able to conduct meaningful research without paying Chronium for AI calls, storage, or expensive infrastructure.
>
> Core research must not depend on a paid LLM.
>
> Paid AI and premium services can exist later as **optional accelerators**, never requirements for the fundamental research workflow.
>
> ### 6. Chronium Stores the Map, Not the Ocean
>
> Follow the bootstrap rule:
>
> **Reference before replicate.
> Index before store.
> Cache before preserve.
> Compute locally when practical.
> Use public/free infrastructure before building infrastructure.**
>
> Original public material should normally remain with its existing host/archive.
>
> User files remain local by default.
>
> Chronium owns the intelligence layer: provenance, metadata, indexes, relationships, hashes, research state, evidence structure, findings, and outputs.
>
> Cloud storage is selective and optional.
>
> ### 7. Let Other Platforms Do Expensive Work
>
> Do not rebuild services that already exist merely so Chronium can say it has them.
>
> When practical, allow users to take Chronium research into external tools for optional capabilities such as audio, video, presentation, visualization, or specialized analysis.
>
> Prefer **user-owned/free external workflows** over Chronium paying for third-party generation.
>
> Later, paid integrated services may be offered as conveniences, but they must not replace the free research path.
>
> ### 8. One Research Engine, Any Subject
>
> Never hard-code Chronium around Copperas Cove, government records, FOIA/PIR, or any single research field.
>
> Those are test cases.
>
> The same engine must support history, government, law, finance, science, education, companies, people, medicine, technology, journalism, public policy, academic research, and other legitimate research subjects.
>
> ### 9. Old Internet + Current Internet + User Sources
>
> Chronium should research across the available evidence universe:
>
> **user files + saved library + current web + historical web + public records + datasets/APIs + archives + other legitimate sources.**
>
> Historical material is a core Chronium capability, but Chronium itself is broader than a historical search engine.
>
> ### 10. Quantitative + Qualitative + Mixed Methods
>
> Chronium must treat all three as first-class research methods.
>
> **Quantitative:** What do the numbers show?
>
> **Qualitative:** What do the records say?
>
> **Mixed method:** Do what they say and what the numbers show agree?
>
> Calculations must preserve inputs and methodology.
>
> Qualitative analysis must preserve supporting passages.
>
> Disagreement between evidence becomes a **review item**, not an accusation or automatic conclusion.
>
> ### 11. Separate Fact From Interpretation
>
> Chronium must distinguish:
>
> **Source Fact** — directly supported by evidence.
>
> **Computed Fact** — mechanically derived from evidence.
>
> **AI Analysis** — model-assisted interpretation.
>
> **Investigative Lead** — something worth researching further.
>
> **Unresolved** — evidence is insufficient or conflicting.
>
> Never silently turn one category into another.
>
> ### 12. Missing Evidence Is Information, Not Proof
>
> Chronium should identify gaps.
>
> Examples:
>
> “Referenced attachment not located.”
>
> “FY2022 record not present between FY2021 and FY2023.”
>
> “Available records do not establish where this amount was ultimately spent.”
>
> But absence of a record is **not automatically evidence of concealment, wrongdoing, or withholding**.
>
> ### 13. Chronium Does the Research Paperwork
>
> The researcher should not have to manually operate Source → Evidence → Claim during ordinary use.
>
> Chronium should build the bibliography, evidence ledger, research outline, findings, gaps, citations, and report state **behind the conversation**.
>
> Those structures remain inspectable and editable for serious researchers.
>
> ### 14. Create What I Need
>
> There is no single “final output.”
>
> From the same investigation, a user can request:
>
> **Outline · Bibliography · APA/MLA/Chicago citations · Footnotes · Timeline · Table · Evidence Matrix · Findings Summary · Source List · Research Gaps · Report**
>
> Outputs must remain connected to the evidence that produced them.
>
> ### 15. Research Should Lead to More Research
>
> Chronium should help answer:
>
> **“What should I investigate next?”**
>
> It may identify missing periods, conflicting amounts, referenced-but-unlocated records, unexplained changes, weakly supported claims, unexplored outline sections, or additional sources worth searching.
>
> These are research suggestions, not conclusions.
>
> ### 16. Simple Enough for a Kid, Trustworthy Enough for a Lawyer
>
> This is the UX standard.
>
> A new user should not need a manual.
>
> The front door should essentially be:
>
> **What are you researching?**
>
> Then:
>
> **Add your sources.**
>
> Then:
>
> **Ask your sources.**
>
> Advanced methodology, provenance, evidence management, and analytical controls remain available underneath for people who need them.
>
> **Complexity belongs in Chronium, not on the user's screen.**
>
> ### 17. Never Fake Coverage
>
> Chronium must tell the truth about what it searched.
>
> Provider unavailable is different from no results.
>
> Not searched is different from nothing found.
>
> A thin corpus is different from a comprehensive investigation.
>
> Never imply exhaustive research when coverage does not support that conclusion.
>
> ### 18. Reliability Through Redundancy
>
> Important evidence should not depend unnecessarily on one provider.
>
> Prefer:
>
> **Original Source → Archive/Alternate Source → Additional Backup → Selective Preservation when warranted**
>
> Preserve provenance through every fallback.
>
> Never silently substitute one source for another.
>
> ### 19. AI Must Reduce Reading, Not Verification
>
> AI can read and synthesize large amounts of evidence so the researcher doesn't have to.
>
> But checking the answer must become **easier**, not harder.
>
> AI outputs must point back to the evidence used.
>
> Do not send an entire enormous investigation to an LLM by default. Retrieve relevant evidence first and analyze the smallest sufficient evidence set.
>
> This protects accuracy, privacy, speed, and cost.
>
> ### 20. The Copperas Cove Test
>
> Use the real Copperas Cove research as a primary stress test.
>
> Chronium should be capable of taking the existing files, public records, budgets, spreadsheets, webpages, archived material and other available sources and allowing the researcher to investigate them conversationally.
>
> The benchmark is simple:
>
> **If it is substantially easier to leave Chronium and conduct the investigation manually or in a general chatbot, the Chronium workflow still needs work.**
>
> Do not solve that by adding more buttons.
>
> Fix the research experience.
>
> Adaptive research depth. Chronium adapts its responses to the user's requested depth, not the user's identity. Same verified evidence, multiple views, Quick answer, explain, research, professional. Users can ask naturally, explain like I'm 12, give me the executive summary, show every source, generate a report. Evidence and provenance remain constant. Only the presentation changes.

>
